This fitness function is based on the concept of "conductance" in community detection.
Conductance is a measure of how well-connected a community is, relative to its size.
A community with low conductance is one where most of the edges are within the community,
and few edges connect the community to the rest of the network.

The fitness function works by first calculating the conductance score for each community in the chromosome.
This is done by counting the number of edges that connect nodes within the community to nodes outside the community
(the "cut size"), and dividing this by the minimum of the volume of the community
(i.e., the sum of the degrees of the nodes in the community) and the volume of the complement of the community
(i.e., the sum of the degrees of the nodes outside the community).
This gives a conductance score between 0 and 1 for each community.

The fitness function then calculates the average conductance score across all communities,
and returns the inverse of this value as the fitness.
The reasoning behind taking the inverse is that we want to maximize the fitness,
but the conductance score is a measure of how "bad" the community is (i.e., a lower score is better),
so we invert it to make it a measure of how "good" the community is.

In summary, this fitness function encourages the genetic algorithm
to find a chromosome with communities that have low conductance scores,
which means they are well-connected within themselves and not highly connected to the rest of the network.
